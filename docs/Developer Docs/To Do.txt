Dynamic Foundry Multiblock
==========================

Core idea
---------
- Working name: Foundry
- Suggested controller id: foundry_controller.
- Important: existing test_foundry_controller was only made to verify that the current multiblock system works. Do not upgrade it into the real Foundry; the Foundry has so many features that it needs a separate, larger multiblock system with more tools.
- Suggested recipe types:
  - create_expansion:foundry_melting
  - create_expansion:foundry_alloying
  - create_expansion:foundry_casting
  - create_expansion:foundry_heat_treatment
- Purpose: a dynamic Tinkers Construct-like multiblock for melting, alloying, holding, pouring, casting, cooling, and recovering metal.
- Should feel like Create: kinetic pumps, shafts, belts, funnels, pipes, blaze burners, tanks, gauges, redstone control, and visible process state.

Multiblock structure logic
--------------------------
- Controller scans a bounded rectangular foundry area when right-clicked, updated, or a block changes.
- Valid sizes should be dynamic, not fixed:
  - Small: 3x3x3 early foundry.
  - Medium: 5x5x4 production foundry.
  - Large: up to a config limit, for late-game alloy factories.
- Required blocks:
  - 1 controller.
  - Heat-resistant casing or bricks for walls/floor.
  - At least 1 heat source below or beside the vessel.
  - At least 1 drain, faucet, casting port, or output hatch.
- Optional blocks add capability:
  - Extra tank blocks increase molten fluid capacity.
  - Extra burners increase heat input.
  - Chimney/vent blocks improve heat stability and reduce overpressure risk.
  - Reinforced casing increases max safe temperature.
  - Cooling blocks, water jackets, or fans improve controlled cooling.
  - Kinetic mixer shaft enables alloy mixing.
  - Kinetic agitator improves temperature equality and alloy quality.
  - Redstone valve blocks allow automation.
- Invalid structure states should be specific:
  - Missing controller.
  - Not sealed.
  - No heat source.
  - No output path.
  - Too many tanks for casing tier.
  - Heat rating too low for current molten metal.

Heat and temperature logic
--------------------------
- Foundry has a current temperature, target temperature, heat loss, and heat capacity.
- Each molten metal/fluid has:
  - Melting point.
  - Alloy melting point is at least the highest melting point of its component materials.
  - Alloy tooltips should show this resolved melting point even when the alloy does not define its own explicit melting point.
  - Pouring temperature.
  - Boiling/instability temperature.
  - Solidification temperature.
  - Heat capacity.
  - Safe mold temperature range.
- Heat sources add heat per tick:
  - Campfire/lava: weak early tier.
  - Blaze burner: standard Create tier.
  - Superheated blaze burner: high tier.
  - Electric heater or induction coil later: precise late tier.
- Bigger foundries heat slower but hold heat longer.
- Bad insulation causes heat loss and wastes fuel.
- Overheating should have real risk:
  - Metal oxidizes or creates slag.
  - Alloy ratio quality drops.
  - Fluid starts boiling and vents.
  - Pressure event can damage weak casing.

Mold temperature and failure logic
----------------------------------
- Every mold has material, shape, temperature, durability, and max safe temperature.
- Mold materials should matter:
  - Clay mold: cheap, low max temp, often single-use.
  - Sand mold: cheap, handles some heat, single-use.
  - Stone mold: medium heat, slow cooling.
  - Ceramic mold: higher heat, reusable but can crack from thermal shock.
  - Cast iron mold: strong, but can contaminate some metals.
  - Steel mold: high heat, reusable.
  - Refractory mold: late-game, very high heat.
  - Graphite mold: extreme heat, fast casting, expensive.
- If molten metal temperature is above mold max safe temperature:
  - Mold melts/breaks.
  - Molten input is lost.
  - Output is not created.
  - Optional: creates slag, smoke, fire, or a hot waste item.
- If molten metal is only slightly too hot:
  - Mold takes heavy durability damage.
  - Casting may succeed with low quality or warped output.
- If mold is too cold:
  - Metal may freeze before filling.
  - Output becomes incomplete casting or metal scrap.
- If mold is too hot:
  - Cooling time becomes longer.
  - Soft output cannot be extracted yet.
- Thermal shock:
  - Pouring very hot metal into a cold ceramic/stone mold can crack it.
  - Quenching a hot mold too fast can crack it or damage the part.

Casting and shape logic
-----------------------
- Shape comes from the mold, not from the recipe alone.
- Existing mold item families should be reused:
  - ingot, nugget, block, plate, rod, long rod, gear, small gear, ring, large ring, screw, bolt, rotor, bearing, bearing ball.
- Casting recipe should validate:
  - Fluid type.
  - Fluid amount.
  - Fluid temperature.
  - Mold shape.
  - Mold material temperature limit.
  - Foundry/drained fluid temperature against mold melting point.
  - Required cooling profile.
- Outputs can have states:
  - Hot casting.
  - Cooling casting.
  - Finished casting.
  - Failed casting / scrap.
- Hot casts should need cooling before normal use.
- Current first-pass drain/caster rules:
  - Seared Drain must be on the side of a Seared Output Hatch and pours into a Mold Caster directly below itself.
  - Drain faces outward from the adjacent output hatch when placed.
  - Drain and Mold Caster use smaller voxel collision/outline shapes matching their Blockbench models, not full block hitboxes.
  - Right-click attempts one pour; constant redstone signal retries automatically.
  - Caster has one mold slot and one output slot; automation extracts product first, then mold.
  - Right-clicking a mold into the caster renders the mold item lying in the caster at 14x14 size, one pixel tall, 15 pixels above the bottom; finished product renders fullbright at the same spot at the same time until it or the mold is extracted.
  - While casting, the Mold Caster should show the molten fluid texture first; after the mold amount duration finishes, the product appears with a short molten-to-solid visual transition, and the mold visually transitions into its HOT_CAST_*_MOLD version during that same solidifying phase when the poured fluid was hot enough.
  - If drained fluid temperature is above mold melting point, the mold and poured fluid are lost.
  - If drained fluid temperature is above half the mold melting point, the normal mold becomes its HOT_CAST_*_MOLD version.
  - Successful casts output CAST_* material items and must be cooled by Create washing into the normal material part.
  - HOT_CAST_*_MOLD items are also cooled back into normal molds by Create washing.
  - Normal CAST_*_MOLD items should not show a Temperature tooltip line; only hot mold states and cast products should show temperature.
- Optional quality system:
  - Perfect, normal, warped, brittle, contaminated.
  - Quality affects durability, processing yield, or whether precision machines accept it.

Alloying logic
--------------
- Foundry can hold multiple molten fluids if a mixer/agitator is installed.
- Alloy recipes check ratios and temperature windows.
- Wrong ratio should produce:
  - Mixed molten scrap.
  - Low-grade alloy.
  - Slag byproduct.
- Mixing speed affects time and quality.
- Large foundries should need kinetic mixing to keep temperature even.
- Alloy examples:
  - Copper + zinc = brass.
  - Copper + tin = bronze.
  - Iron + carbon = steel precursor.
  - Nickel + chromium + iron = stainless-type late alloy.

Fluid storage and pouring logic
-------------------------------
- Foundry internal tank stores molten fluid with temperature per fluid stack.
- Fluids should not instantly normalize temperature unless mixed.
- Pouring modes:
  - Manual faucet into one mold.
  - Drain into casting table.
  - Basin pour for blocks and bulk casts.
  - Pipe output for automation.
  - Measured pour using redstone or valve.
- If multiple metals are in the tank, output should require selecting a layer/fluid or using a mixer recipe.
- Dense metals can settle below lighter metals if not mixed.
- Slag should float and may block pouring until removed.

Cooling logic
-------------
- Cooling depends on:
  - Mold material.
  - Part mass.
  - Ambient biome temperature.
  - Cooling blocks/fans/water jackets.
  - Whether the cast is quenched.
- Slow cooling improves quality for some metals.
- Fast quenching improves hardness for some metals but risks cracking.
- Very large casts should stay hot longer and may burn players/items.

Safety and failure events
-------------------------
- Player touching hot foundry or hot molds takes damage unless protected.
- Breaking an active foundry dumps or destroys molten contents.
- If structure becomes invalid while hot:
  - Foundry enters emergency cooldown.
  - Output and pouring stop.
  - Weak casing may crack.
- Water entering a very hot foundry can cause steam burst.
- Rain exposure reduces temperature unless covered.
- Overpressure from blocked vents can damage casing or eject smoke/slag.
- Optional late-game sensors:
  - Thermometer.
  - Pressure gauge.
  - Fluid level gauge.
  - Alloy ratio gauge.

Automation and Create integration
---------------------------------
- Belts/funnels insert solid inputs into input hatches.
- Pipes/pumps move molten fluids in and out.
- Mechanical arms move molds between heating, casting, cooling, and output stations.
- Kinetic mixer/agitator consumes stress while alloying.
- Encased fan can cool molds or improve chimney draft depending on direction.
- Redstone comparators output:
  - Fill level.
  - Temperature.
  - Structure valid.
  - Casting complete.
  - Error state.
- Display link or goggles should show:
  - Current temperature.
  - Target temperature.
  - Stored fluids.
  - Active recipe.
  - Mold status.
  - Failure reason.

Progression tiers
-----------------
- Tier 1: Clay/sand molds, small foundry, lava/campfire heat, copper/lead/tin/zinc.
- Tier 2: Ceramic molds, blaze burner heat, brass/bronze/iron casting.
- Tier 3: Steel/refractory casing, superheated burners, steel and high-temp alloys.
- Tier 4: Graphite/refractory molds, electric/induction heat, precision alloying and large casts.
